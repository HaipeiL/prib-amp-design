# Abstract

We present a multi-stage antimicrobial-peptide design workflow that separates candidate generation from pre-experimental reliability and panel-alignment screening. Candidate peptides are proposed by M2, an 86.54M-parameter property-conditioned adaptive edit Transformer in which target pMIC and bacterial-species information influence both residue content and sequence-cardinality dynamics. After generation and independent predicted-activity qualification, a PRIB experimental-readiness layer stress-tests a 12,000-candidate frontier using deterministic position-wise substitutions and terminal truncations, auditing lower-tail perturbation response, local activity retention, evaluator disagreement, and sequence-only physicochemical risk flags. A second frozen pan-panel audit then evaluates the same frontier with three independent pMIC models across the ten bacterial species underlying the organizer's 20-strain panel. Final ranking uses a maximin criterion over broad-spectrum, Gram-negative, Gram-positive, MDR, cross-model/species-floor, consensus, and retained perturbation-robustness axes, followed by official novelty and portfolio-diversity gates. The final portfolio therefore favors candidates that are not only high-scoring but comparatively robust across model, perturbation, and target-species dimensions. These are computational screening signals, not experimental MIC, strain-specific MIC, HC50, hemolysis, or safety measurements.
